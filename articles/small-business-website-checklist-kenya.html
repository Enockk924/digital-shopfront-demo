A Practical Website Checklist for Small Businesses in Kenya

A business website should make it easy for the right customer to understand what you offer, decide whether you can help and contact you. It does not need dozens of pages to do that well. A clear, reliable starting point is often more useful than a complicated site that is difficult to maintain.

Start with one clear purpose

Before choosing colours or writing headlines, decide what the website should help visitors do. You might want people to request a quotation, call your business, book an appointment, visit a shop or understand a service. Choose one main action and make it easy to find on every important page.

Explain exactly what you offer

Use plain language to describe your products or services, who they are for and the problem they solve. Avoid vague claims such as best quality or number one unless you can prove them. Include practical details customers commonly ask about, such as service area, delivery arrangements, opening hours or how a project begins.

Make contact simple on a phone

Many visitors will arrive on a mobile phone. Check that text is readable, buttons are easy to tap and the page does not require sideways scrolling. Add working phone, email and WhatsApp links where appropriate. Test each link on a real device before sharing the website.

Build trust with accurate information

Show the correct business name, contact details and location or service area. Use genuine photographs, original graphics or images you have permission to use. If you show testimonials, ratings or project results, make sure they are real and have permission to be published. Never invent customers or performance figures.

Create the essential pages

A small service business will usually benefit from a Home page, About page, Services page and Contact page. A useful Insights or Blog section can answer customer questions. Publish a privacy notice if you collect personal information, and clear terms that explain how enquiries, quotations and engagements work.

Help search engines understand the site

Give every important page a descriptive title and a useful summary. Use headings in a logical order, link related pages together and keep URLs readable. A sitemap can help search engines discover pages, but submitting one does not guarantee indexing or rankings. Use Google Search Console to monitor discovery and indexing.

Plan for updates and security

Keep contact details, prices, service descriptions and opening hours current. Check links periodically and keep copies of important website files. If you add forms, analytics, advertising or payment tools, review their privacy implications and explain clearly what information is collected and why.

Measure enquiries, not just visits

Traffic can be useful, but the business outcome matters more. Track how many relevant enquiries arrive, which services people ask about and whether those enquiries become paying projects. Ask customers how they found you. Use that information to improve the pages that help people make a decision.

A simple launch-day review

Open the website on a phone and a computer. Test the menu, every contact button, the email address, WhatsApp, article links and policy pages. Check spelling and confirm that every claim is accurate. Ask someone unfamiliar with the business to find a service and contact you without help.
