How to Spot Online Business and Job Scams

Online work and business opportunities can be genuine, but scammers also use websites, social media, messaging apps and email to target people looking for income. A professional-looking advert is not proof that an offer is real. Pause and check the details before you send money or information.

Be cautious of guaranteed income

Promises of guaranteed profits, unusually high pay for very little work or fixed returns with no risk should make you stop and investigate. Genuine work usually involves clear tasks, conditions and payment terms. Ask how the organisation earns money and what work you are expected to do.

Check requests for upfront payments

Be especially careful when someone asks you to pay a registration, training, activation or release fee before you can receive a job. Some legitimate services charge for training or tools, but a fee alone does not prove legitimacy. Verify the organisation independently and understand exactly what you are buying.

Verify the company and the person contacting you

Search for the organisation using independent sources, not only links supplied by the recruiter. Check its official website, contact details and registration information where relevant. Contact the organisation through a channel you find yourself and ask whether the person and vacancy are genuine.

Watch for pressure and secrecy

Scammers may say an offer expires immediately, tell you not to discuss it with anyone or pressure you to send money quickly. Slow down. A genuine employer or business partner should allow reasonable time for you to review an agreement and ask questions.

Protect passwords, PINs and identity documents

Never share your M-Pesa PIN, bank password, one-time verification code or account recovery code with a recruiter or stranger. Do not install unknown apps or give remote access to your phone because someone promises to help you get paid. Share identity documents only when you have verified who is requesting them and why.

Read the work and payment terms

Before accepting a project, understand the tasks, deadlines, payment amount, payment schedule, revision expectations and who owns the work. Keep copies of messages and agreements. For online marketplace work, use the platform's official payment and communication protections where required.

If you think you have been targeted

Stop communicating with the suspected scammer and do not send more money. Contact your bank or payment provider promptly if you paid or shared account details. Change affected passwords, secure your accounts and report the incident to the relevant platform and Kenyan authorities. Keep screenshots, transaction references and messages as evidence.
