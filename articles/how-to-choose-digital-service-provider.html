How to Choose a Digital Service Provider for Your Small Business

Choosing someone to build a website, manage social media or introduce AI tools can affect your budget, customer relationships and business data. A careful conversation before work begins helps you compare providers and avoid misunderstandings.

Start with the business problem

Describe the result you need rather than buying a tool because it is popular. For example, you may need a website that generates enquiries, a simpler way to respond to customer questions or a consistent social media presence. Ask the provider to explain how the proposed work addresses that specific need.

Ask for a written scope

A quotation should explain what is included, what is not included, how many pages or design revisions are covered, what information you must provide and what counts as completed work. For ongoing services, ask how often work is delivered and how progress is reported.

Understand the full cost

Ask about setup fees, recurring charges, hosting, domain renewals, software subscriptions, advertising budgets and future maintenance. Find out which costs are paid directly to third-party platforms and which are charged by the provider. Avoid agreeing to a vague price without knowing what it covers.

Keep ownership and access clear

Ask who will own the domain, website files, social media pages, advertising accounts and design source files. Whenever possible, create important accounts in your business name and retain administrator access. A provider may need permission to work on an account, but you should understand how access can be removed if the relationship ends.

Check relevant examples honestly

Ask to see work that is similar to your needs. Confirm whether the examples are live projects, demonstrations or concept designs. If a provider shares performance results or testimonials, ask how they were measured and whether the customer has agreed to the public reference. A new provider may have limited portfolio work; a clear demonstration can still help you assess their approach.

Discuss privacy and security

Find out what customer or business information the provider needs, where it will be stored and which third-party tools will process it. Do not share passwords through ordinary messages. Use account invitations and appropriate permissions where available, enable two-factor authentication and remove access that is no longer needed.

Be careful with AI promises

AI can assist with drafting, summarising, support workflows and repetitive tasks, but it can make mistakes. Ask what the system can and cannot do, whether a person reviews important outputs, how customer messages are handled and what happens if the service is unavailable. Avoid promises of guaranteed sales or fully automatic business success.

Agree how communication will work

Know who your main contact is, how quickly they normally respond, how urgent issues are handled and how you will approve work. Keep important decisions, scope changes and payment agreements in writing. This makes it easier for both sides to understand expectations.

Use sensible payment milestones

For larger projects, consider payments tied to agreed milestones rather than paying the entire amount without safeguards. Request an invoice or written receipt and confirm the payment recipient. Do not share your M-Pesa PIN, one-time passwords or banking credentials with a service provider.

Choose clarity over pressure

A trustworthy provider should be willing to answer reasonable questions and explain trade-offs. Be cautious of guaranteed first-place search rankings, guaranteed income, unexplained urgent fees or refusal to clarify ownership. Compare the proposal with your actual needs and budget before committing.
